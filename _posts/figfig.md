Figure 1 – Genetic loss of EZH2

a. Schematic of Tet-ON PyVmT model: doxycycline induces PyVmT and deletes Ezh2 in mammary epithelium.
➡️ Key: Model allows clean study of Ezh2’s role in tumorigenesis.

b. Kaplan–Meier curve: Ezh2–/– mice develop tumors much later (145 vs ~60 days).
➡️ Key: Ezh2 accelerates tumor onset.

c. Immunofluorescence: Ezh2–/– tumors lose H3K27me3.
➡️ Key: Confirms EZH2 is the main H3K27 methyltransferase here.

d. Quantification of lung metastases: fewer in Ezh2–/– mice.
➡️ Key: Ezh2 is required for spontaneous metastasis.

e. Histology of lungs: smaller lesions in Ezh2–/–.
➡️ Key: Ezh2 promotes metastatic lesion growth.

f. Tail-vein injection: Ezh2–/– tumor cells colonize lungs poorly.
➡️ Key: Ezh2 drives metastatic colonization capacity.

**Figure 2 – Pharmacological EZH2 inhibition**

a. Treatment schematic: GSK-126 after 2 weeks of doxycycline.
➡️ Key: Mimics neoadjuvant treatment design.

b. Lung histology + lesion counts: GSK-126 blocks metastases like Ezh2–/–.
➡️ Key: Drug recapitulates genetic loss.

c. Orthotopic transplant schematic: testing in established tumors.
➡️ Key: Assesses clinical relevance once tumors exist.

d. Quantification: GSK-126 reduces lung lesions, minimal primary tumor effect.
➡️ Key: Drug specifically impairs metastasis, not tumor growth.

**Figure 3 – FOXC1 de-repression**

a. Gene expression clustering: Ezh2–/– tumors form distinct profiles.
➡️ Key: Ezh2 loss reprograms transcription.

b. H3K27me3 ChIP-seq heatmaps: global decrease in Ezh2–/–.
➡️ Key: Confirms epigenetic derepression.

c. Overlap analysis: ~22% of upregulated genes are H3K27me3 targets.
➡️ Key: Many changes are direct EZH2 targets.

d. FOXC1 promoter: H3K27me3 lost in Ezh2–/–.
➡️ Key: FOXC1 is a direct EZH2 target.

e. qPCR + western: FOXC1 upregulated when Ezh2 absent.
➡️ Key: EZH2 normally represses FOXC1 expression.

f. Forkhead screen: FOXC1 selectively increased, others not.
➡️ Key: Specific regulatory relationship.

g. Immunofluorescence: FOXC1 and EZH2 mutually exclusive.
➡️ Key: Functional repression in tissue context.

h. GSK-126 treatment: FOXC1 mRNA up after inhibitor.
➡️ Key: Drug lifts repression of FOXC1.

**Figure 4 – FOXC1 targets**

a. Gene examples: Col15a1, Chl1, Rgma upregulated and lose H3K27me3.
➡️ Key: FOXC1 activates anti-metastatic genes once EZH2 is lost.

b. Chord diagram: targets linked to ECM organization, axon guidance, adhesion.
➡️ Key: Targets functionally block invasion/migration.

c. GSK-126 treatment: induces FOXC1 target genes.
➡️ Key: Drug restores FOXC1 program.

d. ChIP-qPCR: FOXC1 binds target promoters under EZH2 inhibition.
➡️ Key: FOXC1 directly regulates anti-metastatic genes.

Figure 5 – FOXC1 functional validation

a. Invasion assay: FOXC1 overexpression reduces invasion.
➡️ Key: FOXC1 blocks invasiveness.

b. Proliferation assay: FOXC1 has no effect on growth.
➡️ Key: Anti-metastatic effect is not due to slower proliferation.

c. Tail-vein injection: FOXC1-expressing cells form fewer lung metastases.
➡️ Key: FOXC1 impairs colonization in vivo.

d. Knockdown + GSK-126: FOXC1 loss restores invasion despite EZH2 inhibition.
➡️ Key: FOXC1 is necessary for the anti-metastatic effect of EZH2 inhibition.

Figure 6 – Clinical correlation in Luminal B patients

a. Patient tumor staining: FOXC1 and EZH2 are mutually exclusive.
➡️ Key: Confirms inverse relation in humans.

b. Quantitative analysis: FOXC1 mRNA ~ protein correlation; FOXC1 inversely correlates with EZH2 protein.
➡️ Key: Repression holds at transcript and protein levels.

c. Kaplan–Meier survival: high FOXC1 = better relapse-free survival.
➡️ Key: FOXC1 is prognostic in Luminal B.

d. FOXC1 mRNA correlates with FOXC1 target signature.
➡️ Key: FOXC1 program is active in patients.

e. Kaplan–Meier with signature: high FOXC1 signature = better survival.
➡️ Key: FOXC1 target genes also predict good outcome.

Figure 7 – Patient-derived xenografts (PDX)

a. TCGA data: EZH2 high + FOXC1 low in Luminal B; inverse relation also in HER2+.
➡️ Key: Confirms subtype patterns.

b. Lung histology in PDXs: GSK-126 reduces metastasis in Luminal B, not HER2+.
➡️ Key: Subtype specificity of drug response.

c. Quantification: fewer metastases in Luminal B PDXs after GSK-126; no change in HER2+.
➡️ Key: Anti-metastatic effect limited to Luminal B.

d. FOXC1 expression in PDX tumors: up in Luminal B after drug, unchanged in HER2+.
➡️ Key: FOXC1 reactivation occurs only in Luminal B.

e. FOXC1 target genes in PDX tumors: induced in Luminal B, not HER2+.
➡️ Key: Program is selectively reactivated in Luminal B patients.

Figure 8 – Schematic model

a. Diagram showing EZH2–PRC2 complex represses FOXC1 in Luminal B, silencing anti-metastatic genes.
b. Genetic loss or GSK-126 inhibition lifts repression, restoring FOXC1 and anti-metastatic gene expression.
➡️ Key: The mechanism is Luminal B–specific: EZH2 drives metastasis by repressing FOXC1, and blocking EZH2 reactivates a protective program.

✅ Overall flow across panels:

Figs 1–2: EZH2 is essential for metastasis (genetic + pharmacological).

Figs 3–4: FOXC1 is directly repressed by EZH2 and controls anti-metastatic genes.

Fig 5: FOXC1 functionally blocks invasion/colonization.

Fig 6: FOXC1 levels correlate with good prognosis in Luminal B patients.

Fig 7: In PDXs, EZH2 inhibition restores FOXC1 and blocks metastasis only in Luminal B.

Fig 8: Model summarizing the EZH2–FOXC1 axis as a Luminal B–specific therapeutic target.
