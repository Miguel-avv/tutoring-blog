Relationship between fluorescence intensity and colony geometry:
Larger colonies generally contain more bacterial cells, which increases
the total expression of the fluorescent protein. As a result, bigger colonies often appear brighter.
However, the intensity per cell may remain constant—the observed brightness mainly reflects colony size and density.

Why colonies differ in size:
Colonies can vary in size depending on their initial starting conditions (e.g., whether they arose from one or 
several bacteria) and local nutrient availability. Colonies in nutrient-rich areas grow faster and larger, while
those in nutrient-poor or more crowded regions grow more slowly. Other factors, such as local competition and metabolic differences, 
may also influence growth rate and final colony size.

4- b) How could the screening strategy used in BIOL301 be improved?

The improvement comes from using dual staining with streptavidin-phycoerythrin 
(red) and anti-myc antibody (green), which tag different components. 
Streptavidin-phycoerythrin binds to biotin, reporting the level of biotinylation, 
while the anti-myc antibody binds the ligase, reporting enzyme abundance. By comparing both signals, 
we can distinguish whether high biotinylation is due to a highly efficient enzyme or simply the presence of many enzymes. 
This makes the screening strategy more accurate and informative.

5- Look up what a “bicistronic vector” is and consider that you are expressing mNeonGreen mutant candidates along with a 
wildtype red fluorescent protein using a bicistronic vector.

Using a bicistronic vector allows both mNeonGreen (green) and a wildtype red fluorescent protein to be expressed under the same promoter. 
The green signal reports mutant activity, while the red signal serves as an internal control to normalize for colony size, cell density, or overlapping colonies. 
By comparing the ratio of green to red fluorescence, we can more accurately assess whether differences in green intensity are due to true differences in mutant activity
rather than experimental variation or colony growth differences.
